L11a: Foundations of Markov Models and Markov Chains

The L11a material comes in two recordings: the lecture, followed by a worked example. The lecture and example notebooks are in the L11a folder of the week-11 release.

Lecture

How can we model a random process without tracking its entire history? In this lecture, we introduce the Markov property: the next state depends only on the present state. We collect the one-step transition probabilities in a transition matrix, where each row is a current state and each row sums to one. We then compute the stationary distribution, the long-run fraction of time a chain spends in each state, using the power iteration method.

Example

Does a simulated Markov chain behave the way the theory predicts? In this example, we build the transition matrix for a three-state chain and check that it is valid. We compute its stationary distribution two ways: by power iteration, and by raising the transition matrix to a large power. Then we simulate a long run of the chain. We compare how often it visits each state with the stationary distribution, and how often it makes each move with the transition matrix.
